# Avatar photo guide

An Avatar is only as consistent as its photos. Review candidates with the user against this list before any upload or import.

## Rights and consent

- Photos of the user themself, or of a person who has given documented permission for this use on social media. Ask; do not assume.
- No minors, public figures, celebrities, or anyone who has not agreed.
- The user owns or is licensed to use each photo. Importing a Pinterest Pin or holding a download URL does not establish rights.
- If a photo is AI-generated, the user should follow the target platform's labeling rules for realistic AI content.

## Consistency

- One person per Avatar. Group shots only when the persona is clearly the subject and the others consent.
- One styling family: recognizable hair, wardrobe palette and accessories across the set, so rotation reads as the same camera roll.
- One setting family per persona (for example home + café + street), not a random mix of unrelated places.

## Variety that carousels need

Aim for enough members that rotation does not repeat within a deck. A useful starting set is 8–20 photos covering:

- Framing: close-up, waist-up, full body, hands-only or over-the-shoulder shots for product beats.
- Expressions and actions: neutral, laughing, thinking, mid-action (pouring, typing, walking), looking away from the lens.
- Light: daylight, indoor warm, evening, at least one flash or phone-camera shot.
- Negative space: several vertical photos with calm areas at the top or bottom where text can sit without covering the face.

## Quality floor

- Vertical or easily cropped to 9:16; at least 720 px on the short side.
- PNG, JPEG or WebP, at most 10 MiB each (the upload limit).
- No watermarks, logos you cannot use, baked-in captions or UI screenshots.
- Sharp faces, no heavy filters that change the person between photos.
- Passes the camera-roll test: could a real person have taken it? Candid beats posed stock.
